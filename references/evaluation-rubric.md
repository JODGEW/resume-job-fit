# Evaluation Rubric

Use this rubric to evaluate a supplied resume against a supplied job description after applying [evidence-policy.md](evidence-policy.md). Base every judgment on those two inputs.

## Evaluation Method

1. Identify the role's core responsibilities, required qualifications, preferred qualifications, and in-scope explicit hard requirements.
2. Determine each criterion's materiality from the job description's own structure and emphasis. Consider whether it is stated as mandatory, central to the role's purpose, repeated across core duties, or presented as a preference. Do not treat every required qualification as equally important when the job description distinguishes them.
3. Evaluate each material criterion using direct evidence, adjacent evidence, or missing evidence as defined in the evidence policy.
4. Assess the alignment dimensions below, preserving the source, scope, and uncertainty of the evidence.
5. Synthesize the result from the nature and materiality of the alignments and gaps, not from a count or formula.

Direct evidence supports alignment only to the extent actually demonstrated. Adjacent evidence shows relevant transferability while retaining a material difference. Missing evidence identifies what the supplied resume does not demonstrate; it is not proof that the candidate lacks the qualification.

## Qualification Types

### Required Qualifications

Evaluate every material required qualification, but follow distinctions made by the job description. A required qualification tied directly to the role's primary purpose or recurring responsibilities is generally more consequential than a broadly worded or peripheral requirement.

- Direct evidence supports the requirement within the demonstrated context.
- Adjacent evidence indicates a relevant but incomplete match; state the transferable evidence and the remaining difference.
- Missing evidence is an evidence gap; describe the requirement as not demonstrated by the supplied resume.

Do not let strength on unrelated requirements erase a material required-qualification gap.

### Preferred Qualifications

Keep preferred qualifications separate from required qualifications. Direct or adjacent evidence can strengthen alignment or differentiate the resume's relevance. Missing evidence for a preference should be reported only when the preference is material to the job description and must not be presented as equivalent to missing a required qualification.

Do not promote a preferred qualification to required or hard-requirement status unless the job description itself does so.

### Explicit Hard Requirements

Evaluate only resume-assessable job-fit requirements that the job description clearly presents as non-negotiable. Do not infer a hard requirement from ordinary preference language, and do not evaluate legal or employment eligibility conditions that SKILL.md places out of scope.

Direct evidence supports the hard requirement only within its stated scope. Adjacent or missing evidence leaves a material gap or unresolved requirement; describe it without turning it into an application recommendation. If the job description is ambiguous about whether a criterion is truly mandatory, preserve that ambiguity rather than treating it as hard.

## Alignment Dimensions

### Specialization Alignment

Compare the role's primary domain, problem space, and functional focus with the specialization demonstrated by the resume. Use described work rather than job-title or keyword similarity alone.

- Direct evidence demonstrates materially the same specialization.
- Adjacent evidence demonstrates a neighboring specialization with relevant transferable knowledge but a meaningful domain or focus difference.
- Missing evidence means the target specialization is not demonstrated.

Treat specialization as more material when the job description makes domain depth central to the role.

### Responsibility Alignment

Compare the role's core recurring responsibilities with actions and responsibilities demonstrated by the resume. Give greater attention to duties central to the role than to incidental tasks or generic capability lists.

- Direct evidence demonstrates materially the same responsibility in a relevant context.
- Adjacent evidence demonstrates part of the responsibility, a related responsibility, or the same work at a materially different scope or context.
- Missing evidence means no relevant responsibility is demonstrated.

Do not infer ownership, autonomy, production responsibility, scale, or depth beyond the resume wording.

### Experience-Duration Gaps

When the job description states a duration requirement, compare it only with duration that the resume clearly attributes to the relevant experience.

- Direct evidence supports the stated duration and the relevant activity or skill.
- Adjacent evidence shows relevant experience but a shorter demonstrated duration, or does not establish enough duration to support the requirement.
- Missing evidence means neither the relevant experience nor its duration is demonstrated.

Do not derive years with a skill from total employment tenure, assume continuous use, or combine ambiguous periods. If the activity is demonstrated but its duration is unclear, report the duration requirement as unresolved rather than assuming it is met.

For partial date ranges such as year-only `2022–Present`, evaluate the full range of durations consistent with the supplied dates. Anchor `Present` to a stated evaluation date, or otherwise to the evaluation's current date. If every plausible duration satisfies the job-description threshold, the duration requirement may be direct evidence and missing month precision is not material uncertainty. If every plausible duration fails it, report the threshold as not demonstrated without inventing a more precise date. If plausible durations cross the threshold boundary, keep the duration unresolved and surface the ambiguity. Do not infer years of a specific technology or activity unless the resume attributes that duration to it.

### Primary Technology or Programming-Language Gaps

Identify primary technologies or languages from their connection to core responsibilities and from the job description's explicit emphasis. Do not give every named tool equal importance.

Direct evidence must match the criterion at the level it asks for: a listing can support familiarity, while demonstrated, professional, production, or time-bound usage requires corresponding evidence. Related but non-equivalent technologies are adjacent unless the job description explicitly accepts alternatives or defines a broader category. No relevant evidence is missing evidence.

Describe a gap as more material when the technology or language is central to performing the role, and less material when it is peripheral or expressly optional.

### Role-Level Alignment

Compare the role's expected scope, autonomy, complexity, decision-making, leadership, and hands-on responsibilities with what the resume demonstrates. Do not infer level from titles or total years alone.

- Direct evidence demonstrates the material expectations of the role level.
- Adjacent evidence demonstrates relevant work with a meaningful difference in scope, autonomy, complexity, or leadership.
- Missing evidence means the resume does not demonstrate the role-level expectations.

Experience at an apparently higher title or level is not automatically a mismatch. Report a level gap only when the documented responsibilities and context establish a material difference from the target role.

## Multiple Material Gaps

Evaluate multiple gaps by their relationship to the role, not by their number.

- Consider whether each gap affects an independent core responsibility or qualification, or whether several observations arise from the same underlying evidence gap.
- Distinct gaps across central responsibilities, required qualifications, or hard requirements can compound because they limit alignment in different parts of the role.
- Repeated consequences of one underlying gap should not be treated as separate evidence merely because they appear in several criteria.
- Adjacent evidence across several criteria may show broad transferability, but it does not become direct evidence for any unmet criterion.
- Preferred-qualification strengths and peripheral matches do not automatically offset unresolved hard requirements or central required-qualification gaps.

Do not use a fixed number of gaps to determine the overall conclusion.

## Uncertainty

Separate uncertainty from demonstrated mismatch.

- If resume wording is incomplete or ambiguous but establishes a relevant connection, use adjacent evidence and state what remains uncertain.
- If wording is too vague to establish a meaningful connection, use missing evidence and say only that the criterion is not demonstrated.
- If the job description is ambiguous about importance, scope, equivalence, or whether a qualification is required, preserve that ambiguity in the evaluation.
- If an input is incomplete or unreadable, limit the conclusion to the available content and identify the limitation.
- Do not resolve uncertainty in either the candidate's favor or against the candidate.

The strength of the overall conclusion must reflect material unresolved questions. Uncertainty about a central required qualification or hard requirement is more consequential than uncertainty about a peripheral preference, but it remains uncertainty rather than a presumed outcome.

## Overall Synthesis

Summarize qualitatively:

- where direct evidence aligns with the role's central needs
- where adjacent evidence indicates transferability but leaves a gap
- which material criteria are not demonstrated
- how specialization, responsibilities, duration, primary technologies or languages, and role level interact
- which conclusions remain uncertain because of the supplied evidence

This rubric does not define standardized overall fit labels. Do not create numeric scores, weights, percentages, formulas, candidate-specific thresholds, or `APPLY`/`HOLD`/`SKIP` rules.
