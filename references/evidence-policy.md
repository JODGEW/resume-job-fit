# Evidence Policy

Apply this policy only to in-scope job-fit criteria from the supplied job description. Classify resume evidence against each material criterion; do not classify the resume as a whole. Use only the supplied resume and job description.

## Evidence Classes

### Direct Evidence

Resume content that supports the specific criterion as written without relying on an unstated fact or an unstated equivalence. Exact wording is not required when the described work or capability is materially the same.

Direct evidence supports only what it actually states or demonstrates. Preserve its source and context. For example, naming a skill can directly support a requirement that asks only for familiarity with that skill, but it does not directly support claims of sustained use, production use, proficiency, ownership, scale, or a number of years.

### Adjacent Evidence

Resume content that is relevant and potentially transferable but does not establish the specific criterion. This includes similar responsibilities with a material difference in scope or context, related but non-equivalent technologies, and evidence that lacks a context explicitly required by the job description.

State the relationship and the remaining gap. Never silently upgrade adjacent evidence to direct evidence, even when the transfer seems likely.

### Missing Evidence

Use missing evidence when the supplied resume contains no direct or adjacent evidence for the criterion, or when its wording is too vague to establish a relevant connection.

Missing evidence means only "not demonstrated by the supplied resume." It does not mean the candidate definitely lacks the qualification or has never performed the work.

## Evidence Source and Context

Evidence source is separate from evidence class. Professional, project, or academic evidence can be direct or adjacent depending on the criterion, but its source must remain visible whenever it materially affects relevance.

### Professional Experience

Treat work described in an employment context as professional evidence. Limit conclusions to the stated responsibilities, technologies, duration, and setting. Employment context alone does not establish production usage, scale, proficiency, ownership, leadership, depth, or use throughout the entire employment period.

Do not infer years with a specific skill from total tenure. Attribute duration only when explicit, unambiguous dates or statements support both the activity and its duration.

### Project Experience

Treat work identified as a personal, open-source, portfolio, or otherwise non-employment project as project evidence. Classify volunteer work according to the actual context described by the resume, preserving any organizational, team, production, or sustained real-world context it explicitly establishes. Project evidence can directly support a criterion that the project itself demonstrates, but it does not become professional experience or imply production use, organizational scale, team context, or sustained ownership unless the resume explicitly establishes those facts.

### Academic Experience

Treat coursework, research, theses, capstones, labs, and education-based work as academic evidence. It can support knowledge or work demonstrated in that academic context. Do not treat education or academic work as professional experience unless the job description explicitly permits that equivalence, and then apply only the equivalence the job description allows.

## Interpreting Resume Claims

### Explicit Skill Listings and Demonstrated Usage

An explicit skill listing establishes only that the resume claims the named skill. It does not by itself establish how, where, how long, or how proficiently the skill was used.

Demonstrated usage requires resume content describing an action, responsibility, or result involving the skill. It supports usage only in the stated context. Do not infer production use, scale, ownership, depth, proficiency, or duration beyond that description.

When a criterion demands demonstrated, professional, production, or time-bound experience, a skill listing alone is adjacent evidence rather than direct evidence.

### Related but Non-Equivalent Technologies

Treat experience with a related technology as adjacent evidence for a specifically named technology unless the job description explicitly accepts alternatives or frames the requirement as a broader technology category. Evidence may directly support that broader criterion while remaining adjacent to experience with the specifically named technology.

Do not infer equivalence from shared purpose, ecosystem, syntax, vendor, or category alone.

### Ambiguous Wording

Interpret ambiguous resume language narrowly and preserve the uncertainty. Do not choose the most favorable interpretation or turn an implication into a fact.

Use adjacent evidence when the wording establishes a relevant connection but not the full criterion. Use missing evidence when it does not establish a meaningful connection. Classify evidence as direct only when the supported interpretation is sufficiently clear for the criterion being evaluated.

### Absence of Evidence

Resume silence is not negative evidence about the candidate. Report only that the qualification, context, or depth is not demonstrated by the supplied resume. Do not infer that the candidate lacks it, omitted it intentionally, or could not acquire or perform it.

## Guardrails

- Never invent, embellish, or import candidate experience from outside the supplied resume.
- Never silently upgrade adjacent evidence to direct evidence.
- Do not assume years, production usage, scale, proficiency, ownership, leadership, or depth beyond what the resume supports.
- Keep professional, project, and academic evidence distinct when the distinction materially affects the criterion.
- Do not evaluate out-of-scope eligibility conditions or infer them from resume silence.
- Do not create numeric scores, weights, thresholds, fit labels, or `APPLY`/`HOLD`/`SKIP` rules.
